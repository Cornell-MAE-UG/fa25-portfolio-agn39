For our final design project, me and my partner, Matthew Kiewski, tested the effects of competitive stress on heart rate by asking participants to play the Simon game, a memory-inducing game that requires players to remember sequences of colors for as long as they can. We designed and constructed a multi-stage ECG circuit that captures cardiac signals of participants during three test phases. The first phase consisted of recording the participants baseline heart rate, the second phase recorded their heart rate while they simulated playing the Simon game without memory, and the third phase recorded their heart rate while they played the Simon game, where half of the participants were told it was a competition and were incentivized with a prize. We post processed the data in MATLAB to test our different hypotheses.

We ultimatley found that, across the three increasing stress conditions, the percent change in heart rate from baseline significantly increases for participants, but between particiapnts who were told the game was a competition, there was no significant difference in percent change of heart rate.


###### **Hypotheses**

H1 (Engagement effect): Playing the Simon task increases heart rate relative to resting baseline, and the increase is larger than during the simple button-press control.
H2 (Competition effect): Framing the game as competitive produces a greater heart-rate increase than the same game presented without competition.
